Chlamydia and syphilis forecasting prototype

A multi-stage transmission model whose contact rate is estimated from CDC surveillance data each year and forecast by a machine-learning model trained on all states. Forecasts run to 2029, with what-if scenarios, state hotspots and an allocation exercise.

Research prototype. Not for clinical or operational use. In backtests this forecaster was less accurate than the simple rule "next year equals this year", except for syphilis two years ahead, where it was about level. See the accuracy table below. Built 2026-11-18.

How accurate have the forecasts been?

Rolling-origin backtest over the 50 states and DC. Each forecaster saw data through 2019, 2020, 2021, 2022 and 2023 in turn and forecast one and two years ahead. Figures are the median absolute percentage error; lower is better.

An exercise, not a recommendation. Budget: enough extra diagnosis-and-treatment capacity to raise the rate 10% in every state. The cost of a step is assumed proportional to a state's current caseload. Capacity goes, in 10-point steps up to 30%, to wherever the next step averts the most modelled cases per unit cost.

Data. CDC National Notifiable Diseases Surveillance System weekly tables on data.cdc.gov, retrieved 2026-11-19; each year's count is read from the following year's final table. Population: U.S. Census Bureau state estimates. National Inpatient Sample data are not used.

Method. Chlamydia: susceptible, exposed, infected, on treatment, recovered. Syphilis: susceptible, incubating, primary, secondary, early latent, treated. Solved with a fractional Adams-Bashforth-Moulton scheme (order 1.0 shown here). The learner is a gradient-boosted regression model.
